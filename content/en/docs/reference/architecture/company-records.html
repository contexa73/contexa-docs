Company Records: Telling Decisions What the Company Knows

Company records let you put records from your company's systems (approvals, tickets, assignments, on-call shifts, business trips) into CONTEXA. When a request differs from the user's behavioral baseline, the decision also checks whether a record explains the difference. This page covers the records you can put, the requests that use them, the values your application states, what the decision sees, what happens when records are missing, and the API and storage.

At a glance

The same request can be normal or dangerous depending on the business situation. For example, alice exports 1,000 customer records in the early morning. CONTEXA learns each user's usual working hours, devices, networks and targets from earlier requests (the behavioral baseline). If alice has never made a request like this, it differs from that baseline. When an approved approval covers the request, the difference is explained. Without one, the request is suspicious. If CONTEXA does not know your approval system, it cannot tell the two apart.

Company records are how you tell CONTEXA what your systems know: approvals, tickets, assignments, on-call shifts, trips. When a request arrives, CONTEXA finds the requester's records, matches them against the request, and passes the result to the decision as facts. These facts, as the decision receives them, are the business context.

Company records do not decide. CONTEXA only states the facts it found by matching, for example which approval comes closest and which of its conditions fail, or whether the requester is assigned to the project. The AI model reads those facts and chooses to allow, ask for step-up authentication, hold for review or block.

From records to a decision, the order is:

  1. You put records. Your integration or an administrator puts people, approvals, tickets, assignments and company rules through the admin API (Records you can put, Putting records).
  2. Your application states the target of each request. The request address alone does not say which project or customer a request is about, so your application states it as a request attribute (Values your application states per request).
  3. CONTEXA matches records against each request. Only for a request a company rule covers, it reads the requester's records as of the request time (Company rules, Whose records are looked at).
  4. The decision receives the facts. The matched facts go into the decision prompt, the text sent to the AI model (What the decision sees).

Without records, the decision is made from the remaining facts. Company records are on by default, but they do nothing until you put company rules (policies) and people (people). What the decision receives when only some records exist is collected in When records are missing.

Records you can put

Records come in kinds, and you put several kinds together as one JSON document (Putting records). The name in brackets in the table is the JSON field that holds each kind.

  • Valid states. An approval covers a request when its status is APPROVED, GRANTED or COMPLETED; a ticket when it is OPEN, APPROVED, ACTIVE or IN_PROGRESS. "Covers" means the approval or ticket is accepted as the basis for the request. An empty status is not checked.
  • Self-approval. When the approver of an approval or ticket is the requester (their subject or companyId) or is empty, CONTEXA treats it as approved by the requester and marks the record as not covering the request (APPROVER).
  • Items already used. When an approval's usageReported is true, CONTEXA adds up the items your application reported in usage for the approval's target, within the approval's validity and before the request time, and counts them as already used under it. When it is false or empty, the items already used are "not known".
  • The target (target) is the key your company's records use for it (for example project HX-310). Your application states the target of each request (below).
  • Dates. Assignment periods and days worked are compared with the UTC date of the request time.

"None found" and "not looked up"

"No record" can mean two things: CONTEXA looked for the kind and found nothing, or it did not look for the kind at all. CONTEXA tells the two apart for the decision. This applies to six kinds (ASSIGNMENT, APPROVAL, TICKET, ON_CALL, TRAVEL, ACCESS_HISTORY), and the test is whether your company integrates the kind, that is, whether it puts records of that kind into CONTEXA.

  • A kind you integrate with no record for the person reaches the decision as "none found". Example: tickets are integrated and no matching ticket exists.
  • A kind you do not integrate stays "not looked up". It does not mean there is none, so it is not a fact against the request. A kind you do integrate is also "not looked up" when the rule does not use it or the request has no target (When records are missing).
  • Putting even one record of a kind integrates that kind, and it stays integrated after you delete the records. To state that you integrate a kind before you have records of it, name it in sources.
  • The RecordsLookedUp line of the decision prompt states this for each of the six kinds, for example approvals looked up, none found; tickets looked up, 1 found; assignment not looked up, continuing through the kinds.

Company rules: which requests use company records

A company rule states "what a request needs", for example: a request that exports customer data needs an approval. Company records are used only for requests a company rule covers. With no rule, company records do nothing. A rule does not allow or block a request by itself. It tells the decision the standard as a fact, and the AI model chooses the response.

FieldMeaningWhen empty
idThe rule id. It appears on the rule line of the decision promptRequired
statementThe rule as a sentence for people to read. The admin console shows it on the company records screen; the decision prompt does not carry itNone
requirementThe record that must cover the request: APPROVAL or TICKETNo record required. Whether the rule is met cannot be known
requiredTicketKindThe ticket kind needed (for example CHANGE)Any kind
actionsThe actions the rule coversEvery action
categoriesThe resource categories the rule covers (@Protectable(category = ...))Every category
exceptionsWhen no covering record is needed (below). {"kind": "...", "limit": number}No exception
recordsThe record kinds a request is matched against. Choose among the six kinds (explained above)The kinds requirement and exceptions name, with TRAVEL
historyDaysHow many days of work history (days worked) the decision is shownThe RECENT_WORK limit, else 90 days

Company records are used for a request under three conditions. If any is missing, no company records reach that request.

  1. At least one rule matches the request's action and resource category. A rule that sets actions or categories does not apply to a request whose action or category is not known.
  2. The records' holder (below) has a person record (people).
  3. The request time is known. Without it, CONTEXA looks up no records, and the business context carries only a summary saying the records were not looked up because the time is unknown.

Rule exceptions and the company's own check

An exception is a condition under which no covering record is needed. Example: a person assigned to the project exports 100 items or fewer.

Exception (kind)Holds whenlimit
ASSIGNEDThe person is assigned to the target on the request day (not as account manager)Holds only when the request's items are at most this number. It does not hold when the items are unknown
ACCOUNT_MANAGERThe person is the account manager of the customer the request names (a URL variable value) or of the targetNot used
RECENT_WORKThe person worked on the target on a day within the recent days before the request dayHow many days back to look (else historyDays, else 90 days)
TICKET_WHILE_ON_CALLThe person is on call at the request time and has a ticket that meets every conditionNot used

For each rule CONTEXA works out from the exceptions whether this request needs a covering record, and writes the result into the business context. This is the company's own check, and it has three outcomes.

  • It is "no covering record needed" when any exception holds.
  • It is "unknown" when no exception holds but one cannot be decided, because the kind it looks at was not looked up (a kind you do not integrate, a request with no stated target, and so on).
  • It is "needed" in every other case, including a rule with no exceptions.

The decision prompt does not carry this check on its rule line. The rule line carries the rule id, the record it requires and its exceptions, and the model checks for itself whether the rule is met (What the decision sees). The model also chooses the response.

Values your application states per request

Your company's records name a project or a customer by a key your company chose (for example HX-310). The request address alone does not reveal that key, and CONTEXA does not guess the target from the request's text. Your application states it as request attributes. A value your application does not state is not compared.

Request attributeFormatUse
resourceScopename=value (for example projectKey=HX-310). The name starts with a letter and then uses letters, digits and _, at most 64 characters; the value starts with a letter or digit and uses letters, digits and . _ : -, at most 128 characters. CONTEXA drops a value in another formatThe request's target. The value (HX-310) is compared with the records' target. Without it, the comparisons that need a target (assignment, approval, ticket, days worked) are not made, and those kinds stay "not looked up"
requestItemsA numberHow many items the request handles (for example the rows an export returns). Compared with an approval's maxItems and the ASSIGNED limit. Without it, item counts cannot be compared
claimedRecordA ticket keyA key the requester offers as "this ticket covers it". It is not trusted as evidence: CONTEXA checks whether the person really has that ticket and whether the ticket meets the conditions for the request, and states the result
 
// In a controller or filter, before calling the protected method
request.setAttribute("resourceScope", "projectKey=" + project.getKey());
request.setAttribute("requestItems", rows.size());
request.setAttribute("claimedRecord", form.getTicketKey()); // the ticket key the requester typed, when there is one

State the action and resource category with @Protectable(actions = ..., category = ...) (@Protectable). When actions lists exactly one action, that is the request's action. Without a declared action, rules are matched with the action family CONTEXA infers from the method name, the address and the HTTP method (READ, WRITE, EXPORT, DELETE, ADMIN, EXECUTE).

Whose records are looked at

  • A person's own request is matched with that person's records.
  • An AI agent's request is matched with the records of the person who answers for it: the delegator (the person who handed the work over) for a delegated agent, the sponsor registered for the agent when it acts on its own. An agent has no assignments, shifts or tickets of its own. For an agent's request the decision prompt also states whose records they are (delegator, sponsor) (Agents as Security Subjects).
  • Tenants. A person's records are those of the request's tenant together with those put without a tenant (shared by the deployment). Rules and networks are the tenant's when it has any, else the shared ones.

What the decision sees

For a request a rule covers, CONTEXA puts these facts into the decision prompt, the text sent to the AI model.

FactContent
Rules that applyThe rule id, the record it requires (an approval or a ticket, with the ticket kind), and the exceptions with their limits. The rule sentence (statement) and the company's own check are not carried
Closest approval and ticketThe approval and the ticket, among the person's, that come closest to covering the request: their values (approver, state, purpose, target, item limit, items already used, validity, decision time) and the conditions they fail: APPROVER (approved by the requester), TARGET (another target), PURPOSE (another action), ITEMS (too many items, approvals only), VALIDITY (outside its validity), STATUS (not in a valid state)
A ticket the requester namesWhether the ticket named in claimedRecord exists among the person's tickets (NO_TICKET when it does not) and, if it does, the same condition checks
Assignment, work history, on-callWhether the person is assigned (or account manager) on the request day, recent days worked on the target, whether the person is on call at the request time
Network zoneCarried on the NetworkZone line of the request (REQUEST) block: the zone of the company network the request address is in (CORPORATE), the person's trip network (TRAVEL), or anything else (EXTERNAL, only when you put company networks). For a trip network the trip is also stated on a line of its own
Computed at the request timeValues the engine computes itself against the request time: how far the approval's decision time and validity are from the request time, and the items already used under it (the value your application reported, else the items already allowed in the same session). When records are missing

The decision is made even when records are missing or only partly there. What it receives in each case:

SituationWhat the decision receives
No company rule exists, or the request matches no ruleNo company records at all. It is the same as not using company records
The requester (for an agent, the person who answers for it) has no person recordThe same: company records are not used for that request
The request time is not knownNo records are looked up. The business context carries only a summary saying the records were not looked up because the time is unknown
Your application does not state the target (resourceScope)Assignment, approval, ticket and days worked stay "not looked up". On-call and the trip network are still read without a target when a rule uses them
A kind you integrate has no record for the person"None found". When a rule requires that record and no record covers the request, the model sees that the required record is missing. With the minimum action rules turned on, this fact can be one of the conditions of a minimum response (The Decision Prompt)
A kind you do not integrate, or a kind the rule does not look at"Not looked up". It does not mean there is none, so it is not a fact against the request, and a rule that depends on that kind stays in a state where whether it is met is not known

Putting records: the admin API

The admin API and the console are in the contexa-iam module, and every API needs the ADMIN role. Each put and delete is written to the audit log with who did it (COMPANY_RECORDS_RECORDED, COMPANY_RECORDS_DELETED).

RequestWhat it doesResponse
POST /contexa/admin/api/company-records?tenantId=...Put records. The body is JSON holding the kinds above (example below). At most 20,000 records at once; a kind name in sources counts as one. A record with the same kind and key is replaced (the keys are explained below)The number put and the kinds that the put records integrate: {"records": number put, "integratedKinds": [...]}
GET /contexa/admin/api/company-recordsList records. Filter by tenantId, kind, subject, target; newest put first, limit records (default 200, at most 1,000). An empty tenantId lists only the deployment's shared recordsA list of records (kind, key, person, target, content, put by, put at)
POST /contexa/admin/api/company-records/deleteDelete by key. kind is a kind name from above and keys are the keys of the records to delete. Body {"tenantId": ..., "kind": "TICKET", "keys": ["CHG-1042"]}{"removed": number removed}

An empty tenantId means the deployment's shared records. If any record misses a required value, has a period that ends before it starts, or has a malformed network address (CIDR), nothing is put and the response is 400 with a list of problems (problems).

}

In this example, alice exports 1,000 customer records with projectKey=HX-310 on November 3 from the office network, through a method protected with @Protectable(actions = "EXPORT", category = "customer-data"). Your application has stated the request attributes resourceScope and requestItems. CONTEXA matches the records in this order.

  1. The rule EXPORT-NEEDS-APPROVAL applies, because the action (EXPORT) and the resource category (customer-data) match. alice has a person record, so her records are read.
  2. alice is assigned to HX-310, but the items exported (1,000) exceed the limit of the exception ASSIGNED (100). The exception does not hold, so the company's own check is "needed" (this request needs an approval).
  3. Of alice's approvals, APR-7781 comes closest. Its approver is not alice, and its target, action, item limit, validity and status all match the request, so it reaches the decision as an approval with no failing condition.
  4. The decision prompt carries the rule, this approval and whether alice is assigned. The model checks whether the approval explains the request and chooses the response. If no matching approval had existed, the decision would receive the fact that the required record does not cover the request.

In the admin console, Security → Company Records (/contexa/admin/company-records) lists up to the 200 newest records, filtered by kind, person, target and tenant. The screen only shows records and decides nothing (Admin Console).

The key of each kind (records of one kind with the same key replace each other): subject for a person; subject, target, responsibility and from together for an assignment; subject, target and day together for a day worked; id for usage (else subject, target and at); cidr for a network; the kind name for an integrated kind (sources); id for the rest.

Where records are kept

  • With a CONTEXA database they are kept in the ctxa_company_record table (contexa-iam). Without one they are kept in memory and are lost on restart.
  • When your records live elsewhere, declare your own CompanyRecordStore bean. It is used instead of the default store, and the admin API puts records into it. Its methods: put (put), delete (delete), a person's records (ofSubject), the company's rules, networks and integrated kinds (company), a listing (list), and deleting all of a person's records (deleteSubject).
  • When an account is deleted, that person's company records are deleted in every tenant. The company rules, networks and integrated kinds stay.
  • An application's own FrictionContextProvider bean runs beside company records and can add the application's records to the same context.

Checking that the records were really used

The SOURCE_TRUTH check of the evidence audit confirms that a sealed decision really received the company records. For each sealed decision it reads the company records and delegation records again from their stores as of the request time, and compares them with what the decision collected: its on-call record, assignment, approval (approver, item limit, items already used) and delegation. When the account was deleted and the stores no longer hold the records, the check reports them as not kept (Sealed Evidence, the evidence audit).
